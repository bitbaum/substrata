This site maps the things that have to exist before more compute, more power
and more machines can be built. Most of it is tables. This page explains how to
read one.

## Start with one idea

Progress is set by the slowest input you cannot substitute. Not the most
expensive input, and not the one in the headlines. If a data centre can be
designed in months and financed in weeks but cannot be connected to the
electricity grid for four years, the grid connection is the speed of the whole
thing. Everything faster than that is, for now, free.

So the question this site asks about every row is narrow: **is this the thing
that is actually holding others up, and how would you know?**

## The three states of evidence

Every row is in one of three states, and the site shows which on the row
itself. This matters more than anything else here.

- **Verified.** A person opened a source that says so, and the link is on the
  page. Only these are findings.
- **Source found.** Software located a page that appears to say so, and nobody
  has read it yet. Useful, not a finding.
- **Unverified.** Neither. Usually a lead from trade knowledge that is probably
  right and has not been checked.

Roughly half of the maker rows are verified today, and the front page prints
that fraction rather than rounding it up. A site like this is only worth
reading if it is honest about what it has not checked.

## Which numbers are judgements

Two numbers look like measurements and are not.

**Severity**, 0 to 12, is four questions scored by hand: how few suppliers
qualify, how hard it would be to replace, how long new capacity takes, and
whether the buyer can walk away at any price. It carries the date it was judged
and a sentence explaining the call, so you can disagree with it.

**Readiness**, 1 to 9 in the Science section, is the same kind of thing for how
close a technology is to being usable at scale.

Both are arguments. Neither is a measurement, and every page that shows one
says so.

## What the sections are for

| Section | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Bottlenecks | What is holding things up, and how hard |
| Markets | Who actually makes it, and how replaceable they are |
| Policy | Which rules slow or speed it, and who asked for them |
| Science | What would remove it, and how far off that is |
| Capital | Who could fund relief — and whether money is even the problem |
| Calls | What we have predicted, with what would prove us wrong |

## The one habit worth having

When you read that something is a shortage, ask what the binding step is. Very
often it is not the mine, or the factory everyone names, but a purification or
qualification step one layer below, done by three companies nobody writes
about. That layer is what this site is trying to make visible.
